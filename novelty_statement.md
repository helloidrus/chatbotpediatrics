This study proposes a deterministic, constraint-based verification framework as a post-generation safety layer for LLM+RAG-based medical question-answering systems.
Unstructured LLM-generated medical responses are decomposed into atomic, structured clinical claims through a domain-specific extraction process implemented via structured LLM prompting. These claims represent independently verifiable therapeutic parameters, including dosage, frequency, and duration, in a machine-interpretable representation.
The extracted claims are validated against explicitly formalized constraint rules derived from the Pedoman Pelayanan Medis Ikatan Dokter Anak Indonesia (PPM IDAI), a nationally recognized pediatric clinical guideline that was not previously represented in machine-interpretable, verification-oriented representations.
By enforcing deterministic, rule-grounded validation over objectively measurable clinical parameters—rather than relying on probabilistic confidence scoring or retrieval-based similarity—the framework enables consistent and reproducible detection of safety-critical clinical errors in LLM-generated pediatric medical responses.
Unlike prior validation approaches that operate on extracted clinical text, this framework explicitly targets generative outputs, enabling detection of plausible but clinically incorrect values produced by LLMs.
